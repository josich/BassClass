# Research behind the curriculum

This is a summary of the sources used to design the BassClass exercise library. Exercises are described in our own words. No method-book content is reproduced.

## Your two warm-up videos (Scott's Bass Lessons)

1. **"The Circle of 5ths + ZONES = The SECRET to Fretboard MASTERY"** (Scott Devine, March 2023)
   - The neck is split into zones: frets 1–5, 5–9 and 8–12.
   - Play a major triad (R-3-5) on every key in cycle-of-4ths order (C F B♭ E♭ A♭ D♭ G♭ B E A D G) without leaving the zone and without open strings. The zone forces you to use different fingerings.
   - Spend 2–4 weeks per zone, then do minor, augmented and diminished.
2. **"The '3 Rule Fretboard Formula' to Master the Bass"** (Scott Devine & Ian, July 2023)
   - Every triad has three fingerings, defined by the finger on the root:
     - **Finger 2:** 3rd and 5th on the next string.
     - **Finger 1:** 3rd on the same string, 4 frets up (pivot).
     - **Finger 4:** the shape crosses three strings back toward the nut.
   - Exercises: the cycle of 4ths in each zone, then one triad chained through every shape up the neck.

**In the app:**
- "Triads around the cycle in one zone" reproduces SBL's zone tables for all 12 keys and zones 1–3.
- "The 3 triad fingerings" and "One triad, every shape up the neck" cover the second video.
- The zone idea is extended to root notes, 7th arpeggios and scales ("All 12 keys in one position").

## How schools and exam boards order the material

| Level | Typical content (merged from RSL, Trinity, LCM, Berklee, MI, Spanish conservatories) |
|---|---|
| 1 Foundations | Open strings, alternate plucking, notes on E/A, 1-octave major/minor/pentatonic in E, A, G, major & minor triads, whole to 8th notes |
| 2 Elementary | Blues scale, dominant 7th, slides, hammer-ons, 16ths begin, shuffle, keys up to 3 sharps/flats |
| 3 Intermediate | Harmonic minor, maj7/m7 over 2 octaves, dim/aug triads, inversions, ghost notes, first slap, 5/4 and 7/8 |
| 4 Upper-intermediate | Pentatonic in 5 positions, modes in 2 positions, melodic minor, m7♭5, dim7, 9ths, inversions in all keys, 10ths |
| 5 Advanced | Whole-tone, diminished, altered, Lydian dominant, 11ths/13ths, all 7th inversions, tapping, odd-time grooves |
| 6 Mastery | Melodic/harmonic minor modes, bebop scales, approach notes and enclosures, standards (Autumn Leaves, rhythm changes, Giant Steps) |

## Principles every program shares (and the app follows)

1. **All 12 keys, usually around the cycle of 4ths.** Every key-based exercise has "All 12" options.
2. **One position first, then the whole neck.** Hence zones, positions and the 3-fingering system first, then two octaves, string sets and neck chains.
3. **Chord tones are the backbone.** Triads → 7ths → extensions. Lines are built from chord tones, then scale tones, then chromatic approaches (walking-bass modules).
4. **Rhythm is as important as notes.** There is a subdivision selector on every exercise, plus a dedicated rhythm module: pyramid, 16th grid, displacement, polyrhythms, odd meters, gap click.
5. **Measured speed.** Metronome, count-in, a speed trainer (+4 BPM per loop) and best-BPM tracking.
6. **Every drill should become music.** Grooves, progressions and walking lines apply each concept.
7. **Fingering systems coexist.** One finger per fret is the default. Simandl 1-2-4, linear (one-string) and three-notes-per-string are all included.

## Extensions of a simple concept (the "triad ladder")

Taking one simple idea as far as it goes, here is how the app develops the triad:

**Shapes and fingerings**
1. Major & minor shapes.
2. Hear the 3rd.
3. All qualities, including sus.
4. Four types from one root.
5. Cycle of 4ths in a zone (SBL).
6. The 3 fingerings.
7. One triad, every shape up the neck.

**Inversions and harmonized triads**
8. Inversions, climbing and in position.
9. Harmonized triads, any 7-note scale.
10. Harmonized triads in minor scales.
11. … in inversions.
12. … in 4ths.
13. Open/spread R-5-10.
14. Two octaves.
15. String sets.

**Voice leading and progressions**
16. Voice-led through all keys (and by minor/major 3rds and whole tones).
17. Over real progressions.

**Vocabulary for lines**
18. Chromatic approach & enclosures.
19. All six permutations.
20. **Drum rudiments mapped onto triads:** paradiddle, doubles, five-stroke, paradiddle-diddle.

**Advanced harmony**
21. Triad pairs.
22. Harmonized triads in melodic/harmonic minor modes.
23. Sus & quartal stacks.
24. Upper structures over a pedal.

**Mastery**
25. Coltrane major-3rd cycle.
26. Every inversion in every key.
27. Rudiments in all keys.
28. Harmonized triads in all keys and inversions.

The same ladder exists for 7th chords (arpeggios module), scales (sequences, positions, symmetric, bebop) and technique (1-2-3-4 → permutations → permutations across strings).
