### W3. Phrygian (i – ♭II) and Lydian (I – II) vamps: the Neck's two scales contradict each other
- **Where:** Practice → My progression → Neck → Scale.
- **What I saw:**
  - **Phrygian vamp, Minor, Key Am, i – ♭II triads:**
    - `Am · Natural minor (Aeolian): A – B – C – D – E – F – G · next B♭`
    - `B♭ · Lydian dominant: B♭ – C – D – E – F – G – A♭ · next Am`

    A♭ sits a semitone under the tonic. The Am bar shows B natural, against the B♭ chord one bar later.
  - **The same vamp with 7ths (i7 – ♭IImaj7):** `B♭maj7 · Major (Ionian): B♭ – C – D – E♭ – F – G – A`. E♭ clashes with Am7's 5th.
  - In a major key, the same ♭II triad gets B♭ Lydian, which is right. In a minor key it falls through to "dominant that does not resolve" (Lydian ♭7), or to Ionian for a maj7.
  - **Lydian vamp, Major, Key A, I – II triads:**
    - `A · Major (Ionian): A – B – C♯ – D – E – F♯ – G♯`
    - `B · Lydian dominant: B – C♯ – D♯ – E♯ – F♯ – G♯ – A`

    E♯ clashes with the tonic's 5th, and D♯ with the D one bar earlier.
- **Why it matters:**
  - A two-chord vamp is one mode:
    - A Phrygian: A Phrygian, then B♭ Lydian.
    - A Lydian: A Lydian, then B Mixolydian.
  - Round 16 taught the room the Dorian (i – IV) and Mixolydian (I – ♭VII) vamps. These two are the other everyday modal vamps (film and rock Lydian, flamenco and metal Phrygian), and the Neck sends the player to a wrong note in every bar.
- **Suggestion:**
  - A minor key's ♭II major (triad or maj7) is borrowed: Lydian.
  - Add the vamp rules "i before ♭II: Phrygian" and "I before II: Lydian", with the II as Mixolydian.
