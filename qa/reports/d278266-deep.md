## Major

### M1. Headphones (no click heard): at ordinary tempos a perfect take is judged "13 of 15 wrong", because the delay is chosen one note off
- Where: `web/js/judge.js` `judge()`, the delay search:
  - `hits(L)` counts expected notes with any onset within 40 ms, by time only;
  - the estimate is kept unless another delay hits 2 more, and the first such L wins.
- With evenly spaced notes, delays one note apart hit equally many. The estimate (`CLICK_ALIGN` + outputLatency + inputLatency) is kept, or the scan's first lag is taken, and every note is then matched to its neighbour.
- Known answers: perfect takes, 4-string #112 (amp model, ±6 ms human spread), click not heard:

| Round trip | Tempo / notes per beat | Verdict | Delay used |
|---|---|---|---|
| 70 ms (the estimate itself) | any | clean | 70 |
| 120 ms | 60–160, 8ths and 16ths | clean ("even") | 90 |
| 200 ms | 100 in 16ths | **0 of 15 right, 13 wrong** | 70 (estimated) |
| 200 ms | 160 in 8ths | **13 wrong** | 0 |
| 300 ms | 120 in 8ths | **14 wrong** | 70 |
| 300 ms | 160 in 8ths | **13 wrong** | 85 |

- The same at the default 150 ms round trip:
  - 140 in 16ths and 200 in triplets: 13 wrong;
  - 260 in 8ths: 13 wrong;
  - #106 at 200 in 8ths: 4–8 wrong and 1–2 "not heard" on 4/5/6 strings.
- This desktop reports outputLatency 0.04, so the estimate here was 70 ms. The study measured phone round trips of 83–236 ms. A delay 80–230 ms off the estimate is ordinary on wired headphones.
- The speaker path (the click heard) was right in every case: delay 151–152 ms against a true 150.
- Side issue: when the search moves the delay ("even" timing), the result card says "The click's delay could not be measured (**Bluetooth?**)", which is wrong for wired headphones.
- Fix ideas:
  - anchor on the first onset after the silent count-in (note 0 played from silence);
  - or choose the delay with pitch agreement too (run `align()` per candidate and keep the lowest cost).

## Minor

### m1. A string left ringing over the next note is reported as a wrong note
- Where: `judge()`'s fallback when yin's pitch is not the written note. The salience check compares the written note's harmonics with its ±1 and ±2 semitone neighbours, and a scale's ringing previous note is exactly such a neighbour.
- Evidence: planted takes, `perf.ring` on notes 2 and 8 of #112 (the right notes played, two strings left ringing 0.9 s).
- Result: "1 wrong · heard B" for note 9 in 4 of 5 output models (headphones, both speaker clicks, AGC); "unclear" with the unplugged model.
- A beginner who does not mute will be told they played wrong notes.
- Either name it ("another note rang over it") or treat a match with the previous written note as "ok, rang on".
