# Design review · App icon (launcher, PWA, favicon, rail logo) · 2026-10-22

Brief: a NEW launcher icon for BassClass (Android home screen first; also the PWA/maskable icon, the
favicon and the logo in the app's left rail). Modern, in the app's palette (navy #12142a, teal #46c9a2,
periwinkle #6e80e8, peach #e3876e, gold #dba85a), eye-catching and easy to read on a phone home screen.
Round 1 = research and the baseline (the current `web/icons/icon.svg`). Round 2 = the four candidates in
`web/icons/concepts/` (appended below).
(After the review the round-2 candidates moved to `design/concepts/`; the shipped icon is `web/icons/icon.svg`.)

Checked in `web/js/dev/icons.html` (masks, sizes, wallpapers, grey, colour-blind, squint, themed,
maskable, rail, splash), in the app at 800×380, and beside real category icons (Google Play search
results rendered at 48–56 px in my own preview overlay). Not checked: real launchers on devices (Pixel
themed icons, Samsung One UI frames), the Android 16 QPR2 auto-themed rendering of an icon without a
monochrome layer (algorithm not published), iOS (BassClass does not ship there).

Verdict (baseline): the current icon — **REWORK**. A glossy emerald ball with a carved clef that
vanishes below 48 px, no full-bleed background and no monochrome layer; dated Aqua-era rendering.

## What the references say

Category scan (Google Play searches "learn bass guitar", "guitar tuner", "ear training music theory",
"guitar lessons", "metronome": 63 icons viewed at 56 px) and craft benchmarks:

- Play search, bass learning: https://play.google.com/store/search?q=learn%20bass%20guitar&c=apps —
  the bass category is mostly **photo-real bass bodies and headstocks** (Real Bass, Bass Guitar Tutor,
  Bass Guitar Solo) that turn to mud at 56 px, **a letter B** (Bassist: black B on lime; Bass Guitar
  Note Trainer: B in a soundhole), **a bass clef** (Bass Trainer: a clef on a bass neck) and **a
  fretboard with coloured dots** (Scale Master Guitar & Bass). Lesson: every obvious bass metaphor is
  taken; the colours are lime, wood, black and orange — navy + teal is free.
- Play search, tuners: https://play.google.com/store/search?q=guitar%20tuner&c=apps — picks (GuitarTuna,
  Fender, Guitar Tuner & Bass), headstocks (Yousician, Chordify), dials and waveforms. The readable ones
  (GuitarTuna: black pick on yellow; Yousician: green headstock on black) are **one flat silhouette on
  one flat colour**.
- Play search, ear training/theory: https://play.google.com/store/search?q=ear%20training%20music%20theory&c=apps
  — ears, notes, piano keys, clefs and mascots. Functional Ear Trainer (white note on teal) and Perfect
  Ear (white clef-like glyph on green) show that a single white glyph on a flat field reads instantly,
  and also that teal/green + a music glyph is already common.
- iOS Icon Gallery, Music: https://www.iosicongallery.com/categories/6011/ — the curated craft bar.
  Shipped icons use one bold glyph, a simple gradient field and soft volume; clefs appear (Apple Music
  Classical: white treble clef on red), so a clef is Apple's own visual territory too.
- Guitar Wiz (2026): https://www.iosicongallery.com/icons/guitar-wiz-chords-tuner-2026-06-03/ — the
  best transferable lesson: an **abstracted crop of the instrument** (a ring and one sweeping curve on a
  magenta-to-indigo gradient) instead of a drawn guitar. Modern, unmistakable, two shapes.
- Capo (2025): https://www.iosicongallery.com/icons/capo-learn-music-by-ear-2025-09-15/ — one large
  saturated glyph over faint staff lines; the lines are texture (low contrast), the glyph carries the
  icon. Depth comes from a soft inner glow, not specular gloss.
- Apple HIG, App icons (updated 2026-06-08): https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-icons
  — "embrace simplicity… a minimal number of shapes"; prefer **filled, overlapping shapes**; avoid
  extremely thin line weights; avoid text; don't replicate UI or screenshots; let the system add
  highlights, shadows and bevels.
- Android adaptive icons: https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/launch/icon_design_adaptive —
  108 dp layers, inner 66 dp never clipped, logo 48–66 dp, outer 18 dp reserved for masks and
  parallax/pulse, no shadows around the outline, optional `<monochrome>` layer (Android 13+); from
  **Android 16 QPR2 the system auto-themes icons that lack one** with a colour filter.
- Maskable icons: https://web.dev/articles/maskable-icon — key content inside a centred circle of radius
  40% of the width; maskable needs an opaque image; keep a separate `any` icon without padding.
- Google Play icon spec: https://developer.android.com/distribute/google-play/resources/icon-design-specifications
  — 512 px full square, no transparency, no baked drop shadow or rounded corners. (BassClass is
  sideloaded; useful only as a full-bleed reminder.)
- Pinterest (https://www.pinterest.com/search/pins/?q=music%20app%20icon%20design): **sign-in wall**,
  not used. The visible pins behind the wall were 3D chrome music notes and "aesthetic" icon packs: a
  trend, not a benchmark. Dribbble (https://dribbble.com/search/music-app-icon) and Behance
  (https://www.behance.net/search/projects/music%20app%20icon) are dominated by logo concepts (note +
  play button + waveform); they mostly document the clichés. One Dribbble title is telling: "Music Logo
  Without the Treble Clef" — designers themselves steer away from clefs.

## Criteria a winning BassClass icon must meet

Units are the 108-unit adaptive canvas (visible 18–90, safe circle r 33 around 54,54). On a phone
home screen 1 unit ≈ 2–2.3 px (48–54 dp at xxhdpi); in the 30 px rail logo (the rail is 30 px, not 40,
at landscape phone height 380) 1 unit ≈ 0.42 px; in a 32 px favicon ≈ 0.44 px.

1. **Full-bleed opaque background layer**; hero shapes inside r ≤ 31 (2 units inside the 66 circle).
   Hero size 44–60 units across.
2. **Minimum widths:** hero strokes ≥ 4 units; anything that must read at 48 px ≥ 1.5 units; anything
   that must read in the 30 px rail ≥ 2.4 units; gaps between elements ≥ 2.5 units. Thin strings
   (G ≈ 1.5) are allowed only as secondary texture.
3. **Contrast (measured):** hero vs its ground ≥ 4.5:1; secondary elements ≥ 3:1. Meaning must survive
   grey: teal vs peach/gold differ by hue only (1.27:1), so keep the app's shape code (root = rounded
   square, other degrees = circles).
4. **Palette:** navy family (#12142a / #1b1f42) and teal (#46c9a2 / #3fc19c) lead; periwinkle, gold,
   peach as accents; navy + at most three hues. No emerald gloss, no brown.
5. **Brand rules:** roots are rounded squares; E string lowest; four strings in gauge proportion
   (.045/.065/.085/.105 → 1 : 1.44 : 1.89 : 2.33).
6. **At most 3 focal elements; no text or letters.**
7. **Depth:** flat or soft gradient field, one light direction (top-left); no specular gloss, bevels or
   carved recesses.
8. **Monochrome layer:** one flat shape, no gradient/opacity, hero 40–56 units, nothing full-bleed (clip
   lines to r ≤ 30), strokes ≥ 2.4. It must not read as a system glyph (settings sliders, map route,
   keycap, letter).
9. **Glance test:** recognisable in 1 s at 48–56 px on dark, light and photo wallpapers, and under a
   3 px blur.
10. **Distinct from the category:** none of the clichés below.

## Concept directions worth exploring

1. **"Next note"** — two of the app's markers on four strings joined by the dashed next-note arc: the
   app's signature moment ("see the note, see where to go"). Nobody in the category uses it.
2. **"The shape"** — the R-5-8 or octave shape as a three-marker constellation on a navy field, no
   frets: the first shape every bassist learns, drawn the way the app draws it.
3. **"Neck crop"** — an abstracted close-up of four strings (Guitar Wiz-style abstraction) with a
   single glowing root square: instrument without drawing the instrument.
4. **"Root window"** — one oversized translucent rounded-square root through which the four strings
   pass (overlapping filled shapes, as the HIG recommends). No letter in it.
5. **"Four-line staff/tab"** — a clef on four strings (reads as staff and as bass tab). Most legible,
   but it lands on taken ground (see clichés).

## Clichés to avoid

Guitar/bass bodies and headstocks, picks, a lone or beamed note, treble or bass clef, the letter B,
waveforms/equalisers, ears, piano keys, headphones and play buttons, photo-real instruments, glossy
"Aqua" balls (the current icon), 3D chrome notes.

`b-clef.svg`, B2 `b2-clef-aurora.svg`, C `c-root-b.svg`; current `web/icons/icon.svg` as reference. I
also built a spec sketch of my recommendation, **A2** `design/sketches/a2-next-note.svg` (designer's

Bassist, Yousician, Functional Ear Trainer, Bass Guitar Note Trainer, Fretonomy, Scale Master, Fender

Verdict: **A — REFINE, as A2 "Next note"**: strip A to two markers and the next-note arc; it is the only
direction that is both on-concept and unlike anything in the category. B is the legibility fallback; C
should be dropped.

B and A2 tie on points for opposite reasons: B wins legibility and loses distinctiveness; A2 wins

## Findings

### A · a-neck.svg (take forward, simplified)
1. **Too many elements** (Simplicity, Recognition). At 56 px beside one-glyph neighbours it reads as a
   grid or a board game; at 32 px the four markers become confetti. Fix: remove the six frets and the
   fret gradient; keep two markers (below).
2. **Root outside the safe zone** (Platform fit). Lower-left root square: farthest corner at r ≈ 34.9
   (35.5 with its white edge) from (54, 54), beyond the 33-unit circle and the maskable 80% circle. Fix:
   every hero point within r ≤ 31.
3. **Themed icon reads as settings sliders** (Platform fit, Distinctiveness). Four full-length lines with
   round knobs is the system "tune" glyph. Fix: clip mono strings to a circle r 30, knock out 2 units
   around markers, and add the arc so the gesture is not a slider.
4. **Category collision** (Distinctiveness): Scale Master Guitar & Bass is a fretboard grid with
   coloured dots on dark; Fretonomy uses diagonal strings. Removing the grid and adding the arc is what
   separates A from them.
5. Keep: the periwinkle-to-teal sky (eye-catching on dark and photo wallpapers: the sky is 5.9–8.7:1 against black),
   the navy band (brand), real gauge strings, the -20° tilt with E lowest.

### B · b-clef.svg (fallback)
1. **Bass Trainer is the same idea** (Distinctiveness): a bass clef over bass strings, in the same Play
   search; Perfect Ear and Apple Music Classical also use a clef as the hero. Not fixable while the clef
   is the hero.
2. **Same clef path as the current icon** (Concept): to the user it is the old icon restyled.
3. **Dark tile on dark wallpapers** (Colour): navy #1c2045 vs black 1.26:1; the icon becomes a floating
   clef. Fix if kept: lift the radial's top stop to #3a4290 and its middle to #22285a.
4. **Mono too big** (Platform fit): the clef fills the themed disc and the lines run edge to edge. Fix if
   kept: scale the clef to about 0.56 (≈ 41 units tall) and clip the lines to r 30.
5. Strengths: clef 6.7–11:1 on navy, reads to 24 px, inside the safe zone (farthest point, the tail,
   r ≈ 31.5), the F-line-on-D-string detail is a nice touch for musicians.

### B2 · b2-clef-aurora.svg
1. Everything in B, plus: navy clef on a light gradient reads as **printed sheet music**, the most
   generic music-theory image. Strings at 55% navy are 2.4:1 and look like grey staff lines, so the bass
   (metal strings) is lost. The bright tile is the most eye-catching of the four, which is worth keeping
   in A2 (it has the same sky).

### C · c-root-b.svg (drop)
1. **Bootstrap** (Distinctiveness): a bold B centred in a rounded square is Bootstrap's mark
   (https://getbootstrap.com/docs/5.3/about/brand/); the themed (mono) version is literally a filled
   rounded square with a knocked-out B. Your worry is confirmed.
2. **Category collision:** Bassist (black B on lime) and Bass Guitar Note Trainer (B in a circle) are in
   the same "learn bass guitar" results.
3. **Keycap** (Craft): the white 2-unit outline plus the drop shadow make it a keyboard key; a 40-unit
   square over all four strings no longer reads as a marker on a string.
4. **Text in an icon** (HIG): avoid letters unless they are the brand; "B" is ambiguous (note B, initial).

### A2 · design/sketches/a2-next-note.svg (my spec of the change)
Checked at 144/96/56/48/32/24/16 px, three masks, grey, squint, themed light/dark and in the 30 px rail
(themed and rail checked on the first draft of the sketch, before the markers were enlarged). It reads
at 56 px as "two notes on strings with a jump". Risk: square + dashed arc + circle out of context can
evoke a map route; the strings and the tilted neck keep it musical, so do not drop them. A first draft
had a teal halo around the root ("the note now"); it only smudged at small sizes, so I removed it.

## Recommendation

Take **A** forward as **A2 "Next note"**. Ordered changes to `a-neck.svg` (all in the rotated `#fg`
group's local coordinates, `rotate(-20 54 54)`; final positions in brackets):

1. **Remove** the six frets and the `fret` gradient.
2. **Remove** two markers: the lower-left teal root on the E string and the periwinkle 10th on the G
   string.
3. **Root**: teal rounded square `x 26.2 y 50 w 21 h 21 rx 6`, fill the existing `teal` radial, white
   edge 1.3, centred on the **A string** (y 60.5) [final centre ≈ (40, 66)]. Farthest point r ≈ 30.9.
4. **5th**: gold circle `cx 71.3 cy 47.5 r 9.5`, radial #ecd0a2 → #dba85a → #9e7941, white edge 1.3, on
   the **D string** two frets up (R-5, the app's degree colour for the 5th) [final ≈ (68, 42)].
5. **Next-note arc**: `M40.5 47.6 Q49 27 61.4 42.6`, stroke #eef0fd (14:1 on the board), width 3,
   round caps, dasharray 4.2 3.6 (≥ 2.4 units, so it survives the 30 px rail as a dotted curve).
6. **Strings** (unchanged positions, y 34.5/47.5/60.5/73.5 centres): widths G 1.6, D 2.3, A 3.0, E 3.7.
7. **Keep** the sky gradient (#8a9af2 → #5a8fd6 → #3fc19c), the board gradient, the 0.8 top highlight
   and the band shadow (inside the icon, so it is allowed on Android).
8. **Mono**: strings 2.4/2.6/3.2/3.8 clipped to a circle r 30, knocked out 2 units around both markers;
   markers solid; arc stroke 3.2, dasharray 5 3.6.
9. Build it in `iconprep.js`'s layered format, then check: maskable crop, the 30 px rail (add a 30 px
   rail figure to `icons.html`: the 40 px one is not what a landscape phone shows), and the Android
   adaptive background as an opaque layer (replace today's `#00000000`).

Questions for the user:
- The neck is tilted -20° (the app's neck is level). The tilt gives the icon its energy; is that fine?
- Gold for the target keeps the app's degree colours (gold = 5th). Peach would pop more but would mean
  the 6th in the app's colour code. Keep gold?
- If they prefer the most legible single glyph regardless of the category overlap, B (with the tile and
  mono fixes above) is the fallback.

# Round 3 · Final icon sign-off

Brief: the final `web/icons/icon.svg`, built from the A2 sketch with three changes by the main session:
the notes spread apart (root `x25.5 y51 19×19 rx5.5`, 5th `(73, 47.5) r 8.75`), the arc redrawn as the
app's round-dot motion arc (`M43.4 48.6 Q53 33 62.3 43.8`, #eef0fd, width 3.6, dasharray 0.01 5.2, on
a 6.4-wide #1f2349 ring), and a mono layer with gaps around both markers and every dot. Checked in
`icons.html` (with the A2 sketch beside it), in the app's rail at 800×380, and in the rendered PNGs
(`icon-192`, `icon-maskable-512`, `apple-touch-icon`, Android xxxhdpi foreground, background and
monochrome, mdpi legacy).

Verdict: **SHIP.** Both changes from my sketch were right: beside it, A2's arc and square do read as an
open padlock, and the dotted arc is both the app's own motion mark and clearly a hop.

## Findings

1. Optional, not blocking (Composition, Platform fit): to restore the 2-unit safety margin, move the
   root 1 unit along the A string toward the centre: root rect `x 26.5` (was 25.5) in the art and in
   the mono, the mask's gap rect `x 24.5` (was 23.5), and start the arc 1 unit right,
   `M44.4 48.6 Q53.5 33 62.3 43.8`, in all four places (the ring and the white arc, the mask, the mono).
   The root's corner then sits at r ≈ 30.9.
2. Nothing else to change. The faint dots at 16–32 px do not need a separate small-size variant: the
   browser and the rail use the SVG, and the two markers carry the icon there.

## Recommendation

Ship `web/icons/icon.svg` as it is (the one-unit move above is optional polish). Delete or keep the
`web/icons/concepts/` files as the main session prefers; the icon depends only on itself.
